1. EXECUTIVE DIRECTION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Evolve the public website into a polished, customer-first product website
with dedicated journeys for customers, merchants, and riders. Keep forest
green and orange as the brand anchors. Use clear messaging, real product
evidence, accessible interactions, fast pages, and accurate business facts.

Primary conversion: open the published customer app's Google Play listing.
Secondary conversions: merchant onboarding and rider onboarding through
existing, approved destinations. Supporting purpose: company credibility,
responsible-delivery information, and discoverable customer assistance.

Success does not mean copying another company's appearance, adding many
pages without useful content, or creating a second commerce application.
Each new page must answer an audience's questions and offer a real action.

6. PAGE-BY-PAGE CONTENT AND UX SPECIFICATION
----------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Home
Purpose: explain the service quickly and lead adults to the customer app.

Proposed sequence:
A. Header with accessible desktop/mobile navigation.
B. Hero with one clear headline, short description, actual app visual,
   Google Play download action, and a visible iOS coming-soon statement.
C. Simple how-it-works: discover a store, place/pay for an order, follow
   delivery. Confirm the depicted steps against the released application.
D. Three to four evidenced app benefits with cropped, legible screenshots.
E. Trust and responsible-delivery explanation with links to fuller guidance.
F. Merchant and rider signposts, each with its own destination.
G. Short, useful FAQ section.
H. Final download block and full footer.

Draft hero direction, subject to approval:
"Your favourite drinks. From local stores. Delivered."
"Discover nearby stores, pay with M-PESA, and follow your order in the
PourExpress app. Available on Android. iOS coming soon."

Do not imply nationwide availability, guaranteed delivery times, or live
store coverage without verified operational evidence.
Do not put merchant subscription pricing above the customer journey.

6.2 Customer app (/app)
- Show actual customer app screens, not an invented prototype UI.
- Explain only confirmed, released features in simple language.
- Primary CTA: Get it on Google Play, using the exact approved listing.
- Secondary status: "App Store - Coming soon" as plain informative text.
- Provide a desktop QR code encoding the same HTTPS Google Play URL.
- Explain where to find help and what to expect at delivery using approved
  content. Mention Shiriki/loyalty/wallet features only after public-feature
  confirmation and screenshot approval; backend existence is insufficient.
- Include customer questions about Android availability and iOS status.

6.3 Merchants (/merchants)
- Lead with business outcomes: reach customers and manage existing store
  operations, using benefits that the current dashboard actually supports.
- Explain the existing application/approval process accurately.
- Include dashboard screenshots with all sensitive information removed.
- Separate "Become a partner" from "Merchant sign in" if the existing
  application supports separate destinations; verify both URLs.
- Link to merchant pricing rather than duplicating prices in many places.
- Cover licensing/onboarding requirements using reviewed business copy.
- No new application form or new onboarding workflow in this release.

6.4 Merchant pricing (/merchants/pricing)

- Do not advertise SMS, POS/API integrations, 24/7 support, or multi-branch

6.5 Riders (/riders)
- Explain the role, real joining requirements, safety expectations, support

- Remove stale alpha-testing language after owner review.
- Request the rider app's own package/distribution URL; do not substitute
  com.pourexpress.sip or assume the customer iOS timeline applies to riders.
- Do not promise income, available jobs, guaranteed work, equipment, or
  payout timing without approved operational evidence.
- If no public joining/download URL is approved, use an existing verified
  rider contact channel with a truthful label. No fake application success.

6.6 Help (/help)
- Organize FAQs into ordering, M-PESA/payments, delivery, accounts, app

  Add local search only if it actually filters the published FAQ content.
- Explain how existing customers obtain order-specific assistance in the
  current app or approved support channel.
- Never request passwords, TOTP codes, payment PINs, or public ID uploads.
- No new unauthenticated ticket-creation API or inbox integration.

6.7 About and Contact
- About: concise company purpose, approved founder story, genuine people
  imagery if available, and verified company facts.
- Move personal portfolio links away from primary conversion sections.
- Contact: approved email, phone/WhatsApp, and enquiry routing, with honest

- Constrain long-form reading width to approximately 65-75 characters.

Layout and component rules:
- Use an 8px-oriented spacing scale with smaller adjustments where useful.
- Keep content containers consistent, roughly 1120-1280px at desktop.
- Use restrained 12-20px control/card radii; reserve larger shapes for
  deliberate hero/product compositions.
- Provide default, hover, focus, pressed, and relevant disabled states.
- One visually dominant CTA per section; label secondary actions clearly.
- Default to a readable light content area with branded green sections.
  A marketing-site theme switcher is not required for the first release.
- Avoid excessive glass effects, low-opacity body text, pulsing dots,
  large decorative empty panels, and repeated oversized cards.

Motion and images:
- Replace the rotating stock-photo hero with one strong product composition.
- Use approved app screens with sensitive data removed.
- Animate only when it helps explain content; prefer short transitions.
- Honor reduced motion in both CSS and Framer Motion; remove continuous
  decorative route loops and automatic slides in the reduced-motion mode.
- Essential information must remain visible if animation or JS fails.
- No autoplay audio or large autoplay video in the first release.

10. RENDERING, SEO, AND HOSTING STRATEGY
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Recommended outcome: pre-rendered/static HTML for the public marketing
routes, with React enhancing navigation and interactions. Retain the stack
where practical and prove the approach with a small rendering spike first.

Why:
- Route content, titles, descriptions, and sharing metadata should exist
  in the initial HTML, including for clients that do not execute JS.
- Google's guidance recommends unique page metadata and consistent
  canonicals; it also discusses the benefits of pre-rendering [S3].

Implementation requirements:
- One approved route manifest drives titles, descriptions, canonical URLs,
  published routes, sitemap entries, and static rendering output.
- Every indexable page has its own correct initial title and description.
- Exactly one canonical per page; do not ship homepage canonicals on all
  routes and replace them with conflicting values only after rendering.
- Add relevant Open Graph metadata and a real sharing image.
- Keep structured data factual. Use Organization and appropriate page/
  breadcrumb data as applicable; do not fabricate review/rating schema,
  locations, coverage, prices, or availability.
- No iOS store URL in metadata while iOS is coming soon.
- Do not add thin location pages for service areas that are unverified.
- Update sitemap.xml only with published, canonical routes. lastmod should
  reflect real content changes; a sitemap is not a ranking guarantee.
- Ensure robots.txt references the correct production sitemap.
- Preview builds should be access-restricted where possible and noindex;
  robots.txt alone is not access control. Do not carry preview noindex into
  the production release.
- Test direct loads, refresh, browser back/forward, trailing-slash policy,
  old anchors, unknown paths, and missing assets against the actual host.
- Add permanent redirects only for genuinely moved URLs after approval.

Host discovery gate:
- Identify hosting provider, current build command, deploy root, runtime
  requirements, cache behavior, routing rules, and rollback mechanism.
- Do not assume this site uses the same hosting project or Node version
  as PourSADMIN.
- Derive Node requirements from the locked dependencies/host, document
  them, and test the existing package-lock.json before changing toolchains.
- If correct static routes/status codes require host configuration, scope
  that change only to this website and obtain deployment approval.

11. ACCESSIBILITY AND RESPONSIVE REQUIREMENTS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Target WCAG 2.2 AA, verified with automated and manual checks [S4]. A target
is not a claim of achieved conformance.

Core checks:
- Semantic header, nav, main, footer; a useful skip-to-content link.
- Descriptive page heading and a logical heading hierarchy.
- Accessible names, visible keyboard focus, and no keyboard traps.
- Text contrast at least 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text;
  test relevant non-text contrast and avoid color-only meaning.
- Meaningful image alternatives and decorative-image handling.
- Product touch-target goal of at least 44x44px for primary controls.
- Mobile menu announces expanded state, supports Escape, manages focus,
  and restores focus to its opener after closing.
- Fixed/sticky navigation never hides focused controls or anchor headings.
- Route changes update document context and move focus appropriately;
  back/forward navigation preserves sensible scroll behavior.
- Reduced motion, text enlargement, and narrow-screen reflow work.

Test at 320, 375, 390, 768, 1024, 1440, and 1920px widths.
Include short mobile landscape heights, 200% text enlargement, 400%
browser zoom/reflow, keyboard-only use, and a screen-reader spot check.
Test Chrome/Android, Safari/iPhone, desktop Chrome/Edge, and Firefox where
available. Android download must work through an iPhone browser too;
the website must not imply that an Android package installs on iOS.

12. PERFORMANCE AND ASSET BUDGETS
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Record a baseline first; no current performance scores are asserted.

Field targets, where sufficient real traffic is available:
- LCP <= 2.5 seconds.
- INP <= 200 milliseconds.
- CLS <= 0.1.
- Evaluate the 75th percentile, separated by device class [S5].

Laboratory release checks:
- Use repeatable mobile test settings and take the median of three runs.
- Aim for Lighthouse performance >= 90 on key public pages as a project
  target; investigate regressions rather than treating a score as proof
  of field performance or a guaranteed outcome.
- Keep critical content visible without waiting for motion effects.
- Provisional initial-page budgets: transferred JS <= 200KB gzip, critical
  hero imagery <= 250KB, and approximately <= 1MB total initial transfer.
  Confirm feasibility against baseline; document any reviewed exceptions.
- INP is a field interaction metric. Lab responsiveness checks supplement
  it; do not claim a lab Lighthouse result proves a field INP pass.

Implementation tasks:
- Optimize actual screenshots and photography as responsive WebP/AVIF
  where supported, with appropriate fallbacks.
- Include intrinsic dimensions/aspect ratios to prevent layout jumps.
- Do not lazy-load the actual LCP/hero image; lazy-load below-fold images.
- Serve stable local brand assets rather than a live stock-image slideshow.
- Avoid loading multiple full-size hero images on first visit.
- Use efficient logo/favicon/social-image variants for their actual roles.
- Limit font families/weights, verify licensing, and use font-display swap.
- Split page-specific code where useful; remove unused imports/assets only
  after checking their use and preserving user-owned work.
- Inspect cache headers, compression, and cache-busted assets on the host.

19. RELEASE AND ROLLBACK PROCEDURE
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Before release:
1. Save the current website commit and deployed artifact/deployment ID.
2. Keep a known-good build available through the hosting platform.
3. Confirm changes are isolated to this website's project/environment.
4. Preview-test routing, HTTP status, caching, headers, and indexability.
5. Obtain deployment approval and choose an owner-approved release window.

After release:
1. Check homepage and all major routes on mobile and desktop.
2. Follow a Play CTA and verify the intended listing; check iOS status.
3. Check merchant/rider/support destinations without submitting anything.
4. Check existing legal URLs, unknown-route 404, and missing-asset behavior.
5. Check console/network errors and host error reports where available.
6. Record the deployment and send a concise handover to the owner.

Rollback triggers include broken downloads, unusable navigation, blank
critical pages, broken policy URLs, exposed personal data, incorrect
commercial promises, or a serious deployment/routing regression.

Rollback method:
- Restore the previous known-good WEBSITE deployment through its host.
- Revert only approved website-specific route/header changes if necessary.
- Re-test the homepage, legal URLs, and intended download destination.
- Do not roll back mobile apps, merchant dashboard, backend, or database;
  none is part of this release.
- Avoid destructive repository resets; preserve user work and release
  evidence. Deployment rollback is separate from discarding local edits.
